### Introduction

**Forestdrought App** provides daily estimations of water fluxes, plant drought stress and fire hazard for forested areas of peninsular Spain at 500m resolution. Daily calculations are performed using weather data generated by **Meteoland App**  as input for MEDFATE process-based forest model, which also uses topography, soil and vegetation information of each grid cell. Since calculations are model-based, discrepancies may exist between predicted values and measurements in some locations.


### MEDFATE model description

Vegetation is represented in the model as a set of plant cohorts of the same species and similar size, and the soil is represented using a set of discrete vertical layers with known physical and hydraulic properties. MEDFATE performs daily water balance calculations as the result of the interplay between precipitation and the different processes causing water losses (rainfall interception, plant transpiration, soil evaporation, runoff and deep drainage). Model design and formulation details can be found in different publications: [De Cáceres et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2015.06.012); [(2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2020.108233); [(2023)](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-3165-2023). The result of water balance allows estimating the moisture content of leaves and branches, which in turn is used to estimate fuel moisture. The model then uses vegetation structure, composition and moisture of live and dead fuels to estimate wildfire hazard from local simulation of potential fire behavior, using an adaptation of the Fuel Characteristics Classification System [Prichard et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.2737/PNW-GTR-887). The process-based forest model is implemented in the [medfate](https://emf-creaf.github.io/medfate/) R package and detailed documentation can also be found in a [reference book](https://emf-creaf.github.io/medfatebook/).

### Parametrization

+ **Target locations** - Raster definition is the same as **Meteoland App**. Target locations are defined as raster locations overlapping polygons of the Spanish National Forest Map ([MFE25](	https://www.miteco.gob.es/ca/biodiversidad/temas/inventarios-nacionales/mapa-forestal-espana/mfe_25.html)). 
+ **Topography** - Topographic features (elevation, slope and aspect) are drawn from 
a digital elevation model, originally at 25 m, derived from LiDAR ([PNOA-MDT25](https://centrodedescargas.cnig.es/CentroDescargas/modelo-digital-terreno-mdt25-primera-cobertura)). 
+ **Vegetation data** - Vegetation structure and composition at the target location are initially estimated by finding the nearest Spanish National Forest Inventory ([Inventarios nacionales](	https://www.miteco.gob.es/ca/biodiversidad/temas/inventarios-nacionales.html)) plot data of the latest survey (IFN3 or IFN4) with similar dominant species and topography. After that, forest structure at the target location is corrected using available data. Specifically, tree height is corrected using a canopy height map derived from LiDAR ([Modelo digital de superficies vegetación](https://centrodedescargas.cnig.es/CentroDescargas/modelo-digital-superficies-vegetacion-mdsnv2_5-primera-cobertura)), and tree density is corrected by comparing the aboveground biomass estimated for the target location with that of biomass map for the Iberian Peninsula derived from aerial and satellite remote sensing ([Su et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15032832)). Leaf biomass is calculated for each species based on allometric equations (see **Allometr App**). Specific leaf area and other species-specific plant traits are obtained as explained in [De Cáceres (2023)](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-3165-2023).
+ **Soil data** - Soil texture, bulk density and organic matter are obtained at 250m from the global database [**SoilGrids**](https://files.isric.org/soilgrids/latest/data/) and pedotransfer functions are used to estimate hydraulic properties from physical properties. Bedrock depth and rock fragment content were modified using data from [Shangguan et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016MS000686), also at 250m.
+ **Meteorlogical data** - Meteorological variables are obtained at 500m from **Meteoland App**, interpolated from meteorological stations data provided by [AEMET](https://www.aemet.es/es/portada), [Servei Meteorlogic de Catalunya](https://www.meteo.cat/), [MeteoGalicia](https://www.meteogalicia.gal/web/home) and [Red de Información Agroclimática de Andalucia](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/agriculturaypesca/ifapa/riaweb/web/inicio_estaciones).


### Daily operations

**Forestdrought App** calculation engine feeds from meteorology data processed the **Meteoland App** (i.e. download of weather station data and interpolation over raster cells). The forest model is run on each target location using the soil water content of previous day as initial state. The model estimates the water fluxes contributing to the soil water balance and updates the soil water content. After this, the model estimates the drought stress of the vegetation, fuel moisture content and fire hazard metrics. The final soil water content is stored for the next day to be processed.

### Output variables

The app generates different output variables, which we present grouped by categories:

**Soil moisture** - The following variables provide information about soil moisture, expressed in different units:

| Variable | Code  | Description        | Units  |
|----------|-------|--------------------|--------|
| Soil moisture content | `Theta` | Moisture volumetric content, as the weighted mean of the different soil layers. Water volume at field capacity were used as weights | $m^3 \cdot m^{-3}$ |
| Relative extractable water | `REW` | Water available in the soil, normalized between filed capacity [100%] and wilting point at - 5MPa [0%]. | [%] |
| Soil water potential | `Psi` | Water potential averaged across soil layers, as the weighted mean of the different soil layers | $MPa$ | 

**Meteorology** - The most relevant meteorological variables for the water balance model are precipitation (in both, liquid and solid form) and the potential evapotranspiration:

| Variable | Code  | Description        | Units  |
|----------|-------|--------------------|--------|
| Potential evapotranspiration | `PET` | Daily potential evapotranspiration, calculated from the Penman (1956) equation | $mm \cdot d^{-1}$ |
| Precipitation | `Precipitation` | Daily precipitation (including rain and snow) | $mm \cdot d^{-1}$ |

**Evaporative surface** - The most important vegetation variable for water balance is the leaf area index, as it determines several fluxes. Leaf area index is considered constant in forests where all species are evergreen. In forest with deciduous species, leaf area index can change depending on the phenological status of the species.

| Variable | Code  | Description        | Units  |
|----------|-------|--------------------|--------|
| Leaf area index | `LAI` | One-side leaf area of the forest over the soil area | $m^2 \cdot m^{-2}$ |

**Water balance** - The following variables provides information about the different components of the daily water balance:

| Variable | Code  | Description        | Units  |
|----------|-------|--------------------|--------|
| Interception | `Interception` | Rain intercepted by the plants leaves and branches, and evaporated from these surfaces. The model does not include interception losses from snow. | $mm \cdot d^{-1}$ |
| Soil evaporation | `Esoil` | Water evaporated from the soil surface. | $mm \cdot d^{-1}$ | 
| Exported liquid water | `ELW` | Liquid water leaving the forest patch as either surface runoff or deep percolation. | $mm \cdot d^{-1}$ |
| Plant transpiration | `Eplant` | Water transported from the soil to the leaves by via plant tissues and evaporated in the leaves and returning to the atmosphere. | $mm \cdot d^{-1}$ |
| Actual evapotranspiration | `AET` | The sum of interception, soil evaporation and plant transpiration | $mm \cdot d^{-1}$ |


**Drought stress and fire hazard** - For the same soil water content, some species suffer more intense drought stress than others. Relative whole-plant hydraulic conductance is a measurement of the transpiration decrease due to drought stress. The drought stress intensity for each species is defined as the one-complement of the whole-plant relative conductance. Plant water potential is used to estimate relative water content of plant tissues, and from there the live fuel moisture content. Dead fuel moisture content is estimated following [Resco De Dios et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2015.01.002) and fire hazard metrics are estimated according to [Prichard et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.2737/PNW-GTR-887).

| Variable | Code  | Description        | Units  |
|----------|-------|--------------------|--------|
| Drought stress intensity | `DDS` | Weighted mean of the drought stress across different species, using LAI as weights. | [%] |
| Live fuel moisture content | `LFMC` | Moisture content of live fine fuel in relation to dry weight. Weighted average of the different species, using LAI values as weights. | [%] |
| Dead fuel moisture content | `DFMC` | Moisture content of dead fine fuels in relation to dry weight. | [%] |
| Surface fire potential | `SFP` | Index of surface fire potential behavior. | [0-9] |
| Crown fire potential | `CPF` | Index of crown fire potential behavior. | [0-9] |
